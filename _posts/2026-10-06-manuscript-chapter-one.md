## Manuscript: Chapter One

A little while ago, I began writing around the edges of _[She Dressed for No One](/books/she-dressed-for-no-one/)_.

Here is where it begins.

---

### Chapter One

He pressed the hotel door until it sealed, feeling the latch catch with a metallic chink, choosing the quiet click over the hollow slam it might have made. He turned the bolt slowly and raised the swing bar. The room waited.

He stood for a moment, letting the quiet take hold, the weight of the day's small obligations loosening until his thoughts dropped away and only the simple fact of stillness remained.

Clean, bare, functional—beige everywhere, a space meant for rest but not belonging. The carpet muffled sound without warmth; the curtains hung stiffly, untouched. The bedspreads were patterned to hide what couldn't be erased. Nothing carried a story. It was a room that could hold anyone for a night and forget them by morning.

The air handler rattled and sighed, blasting too much cold. He tried three times to shut it off, but the unfamiliar thermostat only blinked back at him, each button press changing the numbers without stopping the noise. The constant rush of air pressed against his skull, static in his brain.

It was just more of the same. All day he had sat beneath the hiss of white noise generators in the office, the machines meant to mask conversation, to keep words from traveling. Voices dulled behind conference-room glass, laughter drifting through corridors from conversations that never included him. Now, in this anonymous hotel, the machinery filled the silence with its own kind of loneliness.

A glint of light caught his eye in the bathroom mirror. For a moment he thought it might be a scratch in the glass, but then he saw it—a single silver hair. It mocked him.

He leaned toward the mirror, bracing his palms on the cold counter. The hair caught the light again—one defiant strand in a head still pretending at youth. He ran a hand slowly through his hair, watching it fall back into place as if nothing had changed.

But things had changed. Just not where anyone could see. The years had thinned his confidence, worn down the need to be known. Being alone had become easier; it felt less lonely than the kind of togetherness that left him invisible. What had once felt like absence now felt like balance.

He could remember the feeling of being certain once—when everything was still in front of him, before choices began to close behind him. There had been a bright future then, full of options, and later a marriage that moved him through the days. Each night he gave thanks for the simple fact of sharing a life, unaware that gratitude had already taken the place of joy.

In those years, pain had never been his to claim. He'd spent them tending to someone else's wounds, keeping himself intact only enough to help. After a while, watching had been the only part left to him.

He felt tired. The weight of divorce, of quiet nights, of coworkers' jokes about how he "still looked in his mid-thirties." And yet, his exhaustion and loneliness hadn't earned him even the dignity of gray. He let out a breath, turned off the light, and the mirror vanished.

The television came to life, light washing over the room. The voices changed with every channel—news, weather, laughter tracks—but all of it sounded the same. Dissatisfied, he set the remote down and sat for a moment in the flicker, waiting for something that didn't come.

He untucked the top sheet on one of the beds, not understanding how anyone could sleep pinned beneath it. Extra pillows came over from the other bed, tomorrow's clothes laid out—a small favor to the version of himself who would wake late.

He turned out the lights and took the remote with him, the plastic weight fitting neatly in his hand.

From the bed, the television still murmured, the channels looping through their din—commercials, applause, the drone of voices that never stopped. Each sound folded into the next. He raised the remote and began moving through the menus.

A grid of icons appeared, rows of bright symbols suspended in the dark. He moved through them slowly, past movies and headlines, until he reached the one that promised endless clips and strangers talking to no one in particular.

Rows of moving pictures filled the screen, small windows offering everything and nothing. A man leaned toward a microphone, whispering into the foam; another gestured beside a row of headphones; a woman held up a shirt, speaking to the lens as she folded it. He let one of them play.

The sound was different here. Softer, closer. Each voice had a purpose, even if it was only to describe a product or fill a silence. He felt his muscles begin to relax. He adjusted the pillows around him.

Each video played into the next. He was no longer listening to the words, but to their cadence, their rhythm. His breathing grew steadier. Deeper.

A new light moved across the walls—soft pink and yellow, the kind that belonged to another time of day. It flickered gently, spilling across the beige until the space settled around him. A woman was speaking.

Her voice carried an easy rise and fall, words shaped by laughter she wasn't quite letting out. He couldn't follow what she said—something about fabrics, colors—but it didn't matter. There was a rhythm to it, a melody that eased the air. The sound of her laugh found him before the meaning did.

The light pulsed faintly, like a signal sent across distance. He watched it fade and return, fade and return, until he couldn't tell if the movement was in the room or behind his eyes.

Something in him had loosened, a quiet he hadn't known he'd been waiting for. He didn't reach for it or question it. He only let it stay.

He lay still, watching the colors shift across the ceiling. His breath rose and fell with her voice without meaning to. The words dissolved, fading to sound, then to quiet. The light softened, the walls dimmed.

He slept.

---

There is something slightly unnerving about letting the beginning out into the world.

Perhaps that is the point.
